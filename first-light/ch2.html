    Physicist's notebook

    An X-ray beam gets weaker as it passes through the body. Each centimetre removes the same fraction, so the intensity falls exponentially:

    I = I₀ × e−μx

    Here μ = 0.045 per cm of water, so 1 cm removes about 4.4% of the beam. The half-value layer is ln 2 ÷ μ ≈ 15 cm of water, the same idea as the concrete and lead layers in Chapter 1.

    Water-equivalent depth. Lung is about 0.3 times as dense as water and bone about 1.6 times, so x counts lung as 0.3 cm per cm and bone as 1.6 cm per cm.

    Why crossing beams work. Every beam is strongest where it enters the skin. Where several beams overlap, their doses add up, so the tumour gets far more than any one patch of skin.

    Tap inside the patient to see the sum for that point.

    Simplified for learning: a 2D slice only, no scattered radiation, no build-up region (real beams deposit their peak about 1.5 cm under the skin, which spares the skin), and one simple μ for all energies. The beam's strength also falls with the inverse square law, using a source 100 cm from the isocentre.
