How MRI sees inside

Spin (Larmor) frequency
63.9 MHz

Hydrogen nuclei in water spin at a radio frequency set by the field: f = 42.58 MHz × B. FM radio stations use 88 to 108 MHz, so the copper RF cage keeps their signals out of the image.

Physicist's notebook

Units. Magnetic field strength is measured in tesla (T). Earth's field is about 50 µT. Our scanner is 1.5 T or 3 T: 30,000 to 60,000 times stronger. Safety signs often use gauss: 1 mT = 10 gauss, so the "5-gauss line" is 0.5 mT.

The field falls fast. Outside the magnet the field behaves roughly like a bar magnet (a dipole):

B = k × √(3cos²θ + 1) ÷ r³

r is the distance from the magnet centre and θ is the angle from the bore axis. Along the axis the field is twice as strong as at the side. Double the distance and the field drops to one eighth.

Why steel flies. Steel is ferromagnetic: the field magnetises it and then pulls on it. The pull depends on the field and how quickly it changes (B × dB/dr), so it rises even faster than the field, roughly as 1/r⁷ for a dipole. An object can feel almost nothing, then a step later be yanked off its feet. Plastic, aluminium, gold and titanium are not ferromagnetic.

Radio waves, not X-rays. MRI uses a strong magnet and radio waves. Hydrogen nuclei line up in the field, a radio pulse tips them over, and as they spin back they send out a faint radio signal. The scanner turns those signals into a picture. There is no ionising radiation, but the magnet and the radio waves bring their own hazards: flying metal, heating, and electronic implants that can be upset.

f = γ × B    (γ = 42.58 MHz/T for hydrogen)

Labels you will see. MR Safe (green square): no risk in any MR room. MR Conditional (yellow triangle): safe only under the conditions on its label, such as field strength. MR Unsafe (red circle): never enters Zone IV.

The four zones. Zone I is public. Zone II is where patients are screened. Zone III is controlled by trained MR staff. Zone IV is the magnet room itself.

Game simplifications: the field uses a simple dipole fitted so the 0.5 mT line reaches about 4 m along the axis at 1.5 T. Real magnets are actively shielded, so their fields drop more sharply outside and pull harder near the bore. Pull values are illustrative, and real safety decisions follow each device's own label and the MR safety expert's advice.
